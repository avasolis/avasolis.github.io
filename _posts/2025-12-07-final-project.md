My "Light-Up Singing Mug Sleeve" is designed to play a tune and light up when it detects that a hot drink has cooled to a certain temperature that is appropriate for drinking. I wanted to create this project because I am a coffee and tea enthusiast. I can't start my day without either and anytime I go to a new place, I always want to try their local cafe. I also am a barista at Goodrich Coffee Bar, so believe it or not, mugs are a big part of my everyday life! I thought this product could be particularly practical because whenever I get a hot drink, which I tend to order around this time of year, it's always way too hot to drink and I need to let it cool. My Singing Mug Sleeve is perfect because it will ring when the mug, which I determined was indicative enoguh of the inside temperature, reaches a certain temperature. 

The mug sleeve is made out of felt. My two inputs were the temperature sensor and a button I made out of two conductive fabric patches, and my outputs were the buzzer and two LEDs. When the button was pressed and the temperature sensor reached a certain threshold, the LEDs lit up and the buzzer played the word "READY" in morse code, which each letter increasing by one note, making a scale. I chose morse code because I really enjoyed the "Help, I Need Somebody" assignment, but I also thought that its sound would be similar to that of a washing machine or an oven that beeps when it's done/ready. 

Materials List (all components are part of the LilyPad USB Plus kit): 1 LilyPad USB Plus Arduino Board, 1 temperature sensor, 1 buzzer, 2 yellow LEDs (specifically connects to A5 pin), 1 small piece of elastic, 1 clothing button, 1 lithium ion battery, 1 sheet of felt cut into two pieces that are sewn together, 1 spool of conductive thread, 1 spool of embroidery thread, 1 piece of conductive fabric (to be cut into 2 smaller patches that will touch and act as a button), 1 needle, 1 pair of fabric scissors, 1 USB to USB-A cord to test code (plugged into Arduino board), 11 alligator clips for testing stage, 1 USB-C to USB adaptor to test Arduino on Mac laptop, 1 mug, hot and cold water 

If I were to do this project again, I'd tell myself to consider that not every mug, to-go cup, etc. has the same insulation and therefore the temperature range is going to be different for each cup. With this in mind, I would tell myself to make the size of the mug sleeve adjustable, meaning potentially adding multiple clothing buttons so that the elastic can attach to the correct clothing button based on the size of the cup. Another tip I'd give to my past self is to draw my paper prototype to scale from the beginning. This would have saved me a lot of time, particularly the mental energy it took with the spacial reasoning part. Finally, I wish I initially made my felt cutouts much bigger. I initially cut out the amount of felt that wrapped around the mug, but then when I sewed in the components, it made the felt less stretchy and made it difficult for the conductive fabric patches to attach. I should have recognized that the components would make the sleeve feel smaller -- you can always make something smaller but you can't make something bigger! Accounting for this from the start would have saved me a lot of time.
